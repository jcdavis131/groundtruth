# Embedding Atlas

**Ask eye about a US place and get a cited, dated public record: where it comes from, when it was read, and what it leaves out.**

A photorealistic 3D globe that fuses live public signals: every aircraft broadcasting ADS-B, ships on AIS, satellites propagated from CelesTrak elements, earthquakes as USGS reports them, open-data public cameras, upcoming rocket launches. And the thing the others don't do: **the water that keeps communities alive**. Rivers, lakes and reservoirs with their live gauges, the aquifers under them, this week's drought, and turbidity computed in your browser from the latest Sentinel-2 pass, with a community water report that shows its arithmetic. And, from the same public sources, **the economy in aggregate**: every harbour on Earth with US port volumes, every US land border crossing with monthly truck counts, countries shaded by trade with their partners drawn as arcs, and jobs, wages, home values and rents for every US county, with a market report that prints its formulas. And **hazards and land**: every current US wildfire perimeter with its containment, NASA's satellite fire hotspots, NWS warnings and GDACS disaster alerts with the severity their publishers give, FEMA flood zones, wetlands with the year they were mapped, and public and protected lands with their owners, managers and easement holders, a space-weather panel, NASA's live stream from the ISS, and tools that measure distance, area and ground elevation. And **terrain and soils**: 3D terrain with no key, USGS shaded relief, slope and 2 ft contours, NRCS soil survey map units with their farmland class and crop productivity index, wildfire hazard potential, land cover and sea level rise scenarios, a click on the ground that asks each publisher what is under it, and an elevation profile along any path. And **infrastructure and geohazards**: transmission lines, pipelines, every US power plant of 1 MW or more, rail, public-use airports, dams, Quaternary faults, landslides, geology and the PLSS survey grid with T-R-S search, AirNow air quality monitors (built, switched off until the operator returns AirNow's data-user form), conflict events GDELT codes from the news, cameras from six transport agencies, and a drawn area that lists what is inside it, watches it for arrivals and departures and prints a land report with its formulas. And **parcels**: lot lines from county and state parcel services, and a click on one for its record as its source publishes it, owner included where the source publishes one, with no search from a name to what anyone owns. And **zoning, permits and licences**: the zoning district ten cities' own GIS services put any point in, with its overlays and ordinance, the building permits seven cities issued in the last 30 days, licensed premises from five registries by trade name, and EPA's Clean Water and Clean Air Act facilities and the Corps of Engineers' permit actions anywhere in the United States. Dark HUD, scanlines, cinematic camera, voice control. Starts with **zero API keys**. MIT licensed.

**Live:** https://eye.jcamd.com (also https://gods-eye-view-rust.vercel.app; deploys from `master`). One hosting caveat: OpenSky refuses Vercel's egress, so the zoomed-out aircraft view there falls back to adsb.lol around the view centre plus the military feed; run it locally or add OpenSky credentials for the full global picture.

## Water: rivers, lakes, aquifers, and what the satellite sees

Four layers and one report, all keyless, all labelled by what they are.

| Layer | What it holds | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surface water** (on by default) | Rivers and lakes worldwide. Below 1,500 km camera altitude: every USGS stream and reservoir gauge in view with its latest flow, stage, temperature, dissolved oxygen, conductance, pH and turbidity; NWS flood category merged onto the gauge; Texas reservoirs with percent full. Each quality parameter is screened against a cited freshwater threshold and the site gets a **screening index** whose formula is printed next to it. Select a gauge for its 365-day trace and where today sits in it. | USGS Water Data API (`latest-continuous`, `daily`), NOAA NWPS, TWDB, Natural Earth 50 m |
| **Aquifers & drought** | This week's US Drought Monitor D0–D4 polygons draped on the terrain. Below 1,500 km: USGS monitoring wells with depth-to-water or water-level elevation and the **national aquifer each well taps** (Edwards, Ogallala, Floridan…). Select a well for its year of daily levels. | USGS Water Data API (`latest-daily`, `monitoring-locations`, `national-aquifer-codes`), USDM via the NDMC ArcGIS service |
| **Turbidity (Sentinel-2)** · ESTIMATE | Below 300 km over water: the browser finds the latest Sentinel-2 L2A scene on or before the mission clock (set the clock back, e.g. say "rewind 240 hours" or run `gev.run("set_time", { offset_minutes: -14400 })`, and it picks the newest scene before that time; the ±24 h slider rarely crosses a revisit), range-reads B04/B03/B08/B8A/SCL straight from the public COG bucket in a Web Worker, masks water, runs the Dogliotti (2015) semi-analytical turbidity algorithm on every water pixel and reports 640 m chips as median / p10–p90 FNU, drawn as a raster and as clickable chips. Where a USGS turbidity gauge sits inside a chip, its latest reading is shown next to the estimate, and its mean within ±2 h of the overpass when USGS still holds instantaneous values for that window, so the gap is visible. | Copernicus Sentinel-2 via Element 84 Earth Search (STAC + COGs), Dogliotti et al. 2015, USGS `continuous` |
| **Rivers and streams (NHD)** | A picture at every height: the USGS **National Hydrography Dataset** as The National Map draws it (streams, rivers, canals and water bodies; refreshed September 2026); while it is on, Surface water stops drawing Natural Earth's generalised rivers. Click the ground for the **NHDPlus V2 reach the point drains to**: its name, NHD type, stream order, drainage area and two **modelled** mean annual flows (EROM gauge-adjusted and runoff-only, cfs; model output, not a measurement; -9999 reads "not published"). Then trace it: **the river path downstream** (100 km), **the drainage basin**, and **the USGS gauges upstream** (150 km). The same three buttons are on every USGS gauge. A river path by flowline, not the HUC-12 chain of "Watersheds downstream". | [USGS NHD tiles](https://basemap.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/USGSHydroCached/MapServer) (browser, direct), [USGS NLDI](https://api.water.usgs.gov/nldi/) and [EPA NHDPlus V2.1](https://www.epa.gov/waterdata/nhdplus-national-hydrography-dataset-plus) through `/api/water?op=reach` and `op=trace` (30 day server cache) |

**Community water report** (top bar → *Water*, or say "water report for San Antonio"): drought class at the camera target, capacity-weighted reservoir storage within 150 km, gauge flood status and quality within 75 km, wells and named aquifers within 75 km, satellite turbidity within 30 km, and a **supply-stress estimate** that prints its weights, its normalised terms and which terms were missing. Every line links to the feature it came from.

What the turbidity layer is and is not. It is the physics "teacher" that the TurbidityVision project (a separate, distilled LightGBM model; its explainer is the reference for the recipe below) learns from: T = A·ρw / (1 − ρw/C) with Dogliotti's published red (A 228.1, C 0.1641) and NIR (A 3078.9, C 0.2112) coefficients, blended between ρ_red 0.05 and 0.07, on the water mask SCL 6 ∪ (NDWI > 0.05 ∧ ρB08 < 0.10) minus cloud, shadow, snow and saturated classes. The distilled model's boosters are not bundled here. The numbers are estimates of a physical quantity from reflectance, not measurements, not regulatory values; the chip dossier says so, the layer tag says so, and the p10–p90 is the spread *inside* the chip, not model uncertainty.

Validation before shipping: the pipeline in `lib/water/` was run on the explainer's own Bexar County scene `S2A_14RNT_20250118_0_L2A` and reproduced its per-reservoir medians (Calaveras 4.47 vs 4.44 FNU, Braunig 4.02 vs 4.04, Mitchell Lake 15.98 vs 15.7). The same run settled the DN→reflectance question empirically: on this bucket ρ = DN/10000 passes the dark-water / land-NDVI / negative-fraction check and the STAC `offset −0.1` metadata does not (70 % negative reflectances), so the worker tries both conventions on every scene, keeps the one that passes and prints the check in the dossier.

## Trade, commerce and home values

Three more layers and a second report. Same rules: keyless, published values only, estimates labelled with their arithmetic. These three are **aggregates** by design: counties, metros, states, harbours and ports of entry.

| Layer | What it holds | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ports & trade** | 3,807 harbours from the NGA World Port Index with size, harbour type, channel and pier depths, facilities and LOCODE (large ones from orbit, every one below 250 km). 45 of the 55 port authorities in the BTS Port Performance dataset (the ones that name a harbour city; ten river districts are left off) carry its statistics: container TEU and total tonnage by year with the national rank, imports / exports / empties, top commodities, vessel calls. Every US land port of entry with 25 months of trucks, trains, buses, personal vehicles and pedestrians (BTS Border Crossing Entry Data, latest month). Countries shaded by goods-and-services trade with GDP, exports, imports, trade share of GDP and container throughput from the World Bank; select a country and its top export markets and import sources arrive as arcs from WITS TradeStats. | NGA Pub 150 (bundled snapshot, `scripts/economy-data.mjs` refreshes it), data.bts.gov, World Bank WDI + WITS, Natural Earth 110 m |
| **Jobs & wages** | Every county (states above 2,500 km): establishments, third-month employment, total quarterly wages and the average weekly wage from the BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages for the newest quarter published, with BLS's own over-the-year changes. Glyphs colour by the jobs change; select a county for its private-sector NAICS mix with location quotients. Cells BLS withholds stay blank. | BLS QCEW open-data CSV (`industry/10` for every area, `area/<fips>` on selection), Census TIGERweb generalized polygons |
| **Home values** · ESTIMATE | Every county Zillow models (about 3,070) filled by the one-year change in the typical home value, with the five-year change, the typical rent where ZORI covers it (about 1,390 counties), price-to-rent, and on selection a ten-year trace against the national value plus a **mortgage-against-wages** line: principal and interest on the typical home at this week's Freddie Mac rate, as a share of one average job's pay in that county, with the formula printed. | Zillow Research ZHVI and ZORI public CSVs (data through Zillow), FRED `MORTGAGE30US`, Census TIGERweb |

**Market report** (top bar → *Market*, or say "market report for Austin"): the county under the camera target with its home value and rent changes against the metro and the nation, the affordability estimate, jobs and wages with the sectors concentrated there, the ports and border crossings within 200–250 km ranked by volume, a national pulse from FRED and the BTS supply-chain indicators (mortgage rate, unemployment, retail sales, trade balance, housing starts, Case-Shiller, containerized imports, Shanghai→LA box rate, ships waiting for a berth, diesel), and a **momentum index**: a weighted mean of the published one-year changes in home values, rents, jobs and wages, each clipped to a stated scale, weights printed, missing terms listed. It is an index of published changes, not a forecast and not advice.

Where the numbers come from and what was checked before shipping: the Census Bureau's data API now requires a key, so nothing here uses it; county geometry comes from TIGERweb's generalized services, which answered a Texas-sized box (429 counties at 1:20M) in about half a second. BLS QCEW answered 2026 Q1 for all 3,275 county totals with no suppressed county totals (sector cells can be). Zillow's county file carried a July 2026 value for all 3,071 counties and a five-year comparison for 3,020. BTS port authorities were placed by matching their harbour city to a World Port Index entry within 150 km of the geocoded city; the four with no WPI entry (Palm Beach district, Pittsburgh, South Louisiana, St. Louis) sit at the city and say so in the dossier; ten river districts that name no city are left off. WITS's newest bilateral year was 2023 at the time of writing, and the arcs say which year they are.

## Hazards & land

Six more layers, a panel and three tools. Same rules: keyless, each value as its publisher sent it, and every severity in the publisher's own words. A source that rates nothing (EONET volcanoes, an NWS alert marked "Unknown", FEMA zone D) is drawn in its own "not rated" violet and says so; it is never drawn as calm.

| Layer | What it holds | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wildfires (US)** | Every current US wildland fire in the interagency system: perimeters with acres, percent contained, discovery date and **how old the perimeter is**, counted from the time WFIGS says the perimeter was captured (about a quarter of current perimeters publish no capture time; those say so rather than borrow the record's edit time, which can run weeks later), and the incident point for fires with no perimeter yet. The "current" services keep a fire until it is declared contained, controlled or out, so a 100 %-contained fire can still show; containment is on every feature and a fire that reports none says "not reported", never 0. Prescribed fires are violet. | NIFC WFIGS Current Interagency Fire Perimeters + Current Wildland Fire Incident Locations (IRWIN) |
| **Active fires** | Every thermal hotspot NASA's satellites saw in the last 24 hours, worldwide: VIIRS 375 m (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20) and MODIS 1 km (Terra, Aqua), with fire radiative power and acquisition time. The server reads the three 24 h files (about 19 MB) every 30 minutes and cuts them to the view; above 5,000 in a view they are binned and each cell shows its brightest detection with the count it stands for; the field, the strata rail and a construct's "inside" list count a cell as those detections, not as one. **Confidence stays on each instrument's scale** (VIIRS low / nominal / high, MODIS 0–100 %). A hotspot is a hot pixel, not a confirmed fire. | NASA LANCE FIRMS global 24 h CSVs |
| **Hazard alerts** | Active NWS watches, warnings and advisories with severity, urgency and certainty; every event GDACS marks current worldwide (cyclones, floods, droughts, volcanoes, wildfires, earthquakes) with GDACS's Green / Orange / Red level, Orange and Red labelled on the globe; NASA EONET open volcano events. An NWS alert is drawn as the polygon NWS published, or else as the counties its SAME codes list (and says so); marine and zone-only alerts with no county are counted in the layer note, not drawn. GDACS's RSS feed is the list (it carries every event type and says which are current; ended ones are left out), merged with GDACS's latest-100 list and a search for current Orange and Red events, so the highest-rated can never fall off a capped list; the note says "GDACS list may be incomplete" when it might be. Two other layers draw some of the same events, and this one steps aside only while they are on and drawing the event themselves: a GDACS quake that the USGS 24 h feed also has (within 100 km and 30 min) is left to the Earthquakes layer, and only when GDACS rates it Green (Orange, Red and unrated quakes always stay); an NWS alert rated Severe or Extreme is left to **Live warnings** (the constructs layer below, whose feed asks NWS for exactly those) only when that feed outlined it (the server marks it `liveOutlined`) and Live warnings holds it on the map right now. An alert Live warnings could not outline (its zone lookups are capped), a live feed that failed, and a Live warnings layer that is erroring or still loading all leave the alert here, so a warning is never hidden from both layers. Moderate, Minor and unrated NWS alerts always stay here, and with both layers off every event is drawn here; the layer note counts what was left to whom and what stayed, and why. Toggling either layer re-sorts what is already loaded, without a refetch. Each source gets 20 s; a slow one is left out of that answer (and named) rather than holding up the rest. | NWS `alerts/active`, GDACS RSS + `geteventlist/EVENTS4APP` + `geteventlist/SEARCH`, NASA EONET v3, Census TIGERweb 1:20M counties, USGS `all_day` (the Earthquakes layer's own feed) for the quake check |
| **Flood zones (FEMA)** · regulatory map | Below 5 km: FEMA flood hazard zones (AE, A, AO, VE, X…) with FEMA's own meaning of each, whether the ground is in the special flood hazard area, and the **base flood elevation** where FEMA published one (the −9999 "none" sentinel is dropped, not shown). Zones load for a box of about 9 km around the view, drawn as a **dashed outline**: outside it no zone is loaded to click, which is not the same as "no flood zone". Under the zones, FEMA's own rendering of the same layer fills the whole view, a picture with nothing to click. A regulatory map of the 1 % annual-chance floodplain, **not a forecast**; the Surface water layer's NWS flood categories are the "is it flooding now" answer. | FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer, MapServer layer 28 |
| **Wetlands** | Below 10 km: wetland and deep-water polygons with their Cowardin code (e.g. PEM1A), plain type, system, class, water regime and acres, coloured like the NWI Wetlands Mapper, inside the same dashed box as flood zones. **Every polygon says when it was mapped**: the year, date, scale and type of the imagery its NWI mapping project was drawn from, which can be decades old (around Mitchell Lake, 1983 colour-infrared at 1:58,000), with USFWS's own caveat that "wetlands or other mapped features may have changed since the date of the imagery and/or field work". Above 10 km the layer draws NWI's national raster instead (USFWS's Wetlands_Raster service, about 100 m pixels in eight wetland types, NoData clear), so the inventory shows from orbit; small wetlands can be missing at that scale, and the raster has no click answer yet. A habitat inventory from imagery, not a jurisdictional delineation. | USFWS National Wetlands Inventory, Wetlands (layer 0; Cowardin names decoded from each polygon's code) and Data_Source (Image Year) map services |
| **Impaired waters (EPA 303(d))** | Below 10 km, inside a dashed box: the assessment units states, territories and tribes **report to EPA as impaired** (EPA ATTAINS), streams as lines and lakes, estuaries and watersheds as areas, red where on the **303(d) list** (category 5) and orange where a TMDL or other plan is in place (category 4). Each record gives the unit's name and id, the category and what it means, 303(d) listing and TMDL ("not reported" when absent, never "no"), the reporting cycle, overall status, **EPA's action status** (about 54% of impaired line records are a state's submittal EPA has not acted on yet) and the unit's How's My Waterway page. A listing for the whole unit and one cycle, **not a measurement at the point**; waters not shown were not reported impaired, which includes waters never assessed and is not the same as clean. Nothing draws above 10 km: EPA's zoomed-out map timed out too often when probed. Found in ⌘K by assessment unit id. | U.S. EPA ATTAINS, `ATTAINS_Assessment` FeatureServer (layers 1 and 2, `isimpaired='Y'`) |
| **PFAS in drinking water (EPA UCMR 5)** | Below 25 km: the public water systems that serve the view, with their results from **EPA's fifth Unregulated Contaminant Monitoring Rule** (2023-2025, 29 PFAS). Below 10 km each system is drawn on its **service area** as EPA maps it (a boundary EPA modelled is dashed and says so); otherwise, and for a system with no boundary, as a **ring** at EPA's estimated location, labelled with how EPA placed it, **never a pin**. Each system is in one of four states: PFAS **detected** at or above the reporting level (filled by its highest single result: under 5, 5-10, 10-20, 20+ ng/L, a measured value, **not compared with any health limit**), **sampled with none** at or above it (outlined), **not in UCMR 5** (dashed; most systems serving under 3,300 people were sampled only as a representative 800), or **unknown** when EPA did not answer (never drawn as none). The record lists each PFAS with its highest result and how many results reached the reporting level. Samples are taken where treated water enters the distribution system, **not at a tap**, and are not a statement about water quality today. Hazard Index rows and UCMR 3 are not used. Found in ⌘K by PWSID; a system's name (some are apartment complexes or rental parks) is never searched. | EPA PFAS Analytic Tools (`PFAS_Analytic_Tools_Layers` layer 1, UCMR 5 rows) and EPA Community Water System Service Area Boundaries |
| **Historic places (NRHP)** · public listings only | Below 25 km: properties listed on the **National Register of Historic Places** as the National Park Service's public spatial data shows them, coloured by type (building, structure, district, site, object), National Historic Landmarks drawn larger; below 10 km the boundaries of districts and sites too, inside a dashed box. Each dossier gives the property's name as NPS lists it, the type, the NHL flag, the listing month, city, county and state, the NRIS reference number and the nomination at the National Archives. **Only what NPS publishes as non-restricted**: archaeological sites whose locations are withheld under NHPA section 304 and ARPA are not in the source, so absence on the map is not absence on the ground, and this is not a Section 106 review or a SHPO file search. No address is requested; a listing is found in ⌘K by its NRIS number, never by its name, which can be a person's. A **Landmarks only** chip under the row narrows it to National Historic Landmarks (NPS's Is_NHL flag, 1,196 points), with the same exclusions. | NPS Cultural Resources GIS, `nrhp_locations` map service (layers 0 and 1, STATUS 'Listed') |
| **National heritage (NPS)** | **National Park System units** (437: centroids from afar, NPS's authorised boundaries below 250 km, with type, state, region, the boundary's edit date and a link to the unit's nps.gov page), the 62 **National Heritage Areas** with NPS's own description, **American Battlefield Protection Program** study and core areas of the Civil War, Revolutionary War and War of 1812 (below 1,500 km; study areas outlined, core areas filled, by war) and **World Heritage Sites** with an NPS boundary, one per inscription (the nine Frank Lloyd Wright buildings are one). Designations, not ownership: for who owns the ground, see Public lands; most battlefield land is private, and a heritage area is a partnership region, not federal land. | Four NPS services (Land Resources Division, Heritage Partnerships, ABPP, World Heritage) through `/api/land?op=heritage` (1 day for boxes, 7 days for national answers) |
| **Critical habitat (ESA)** · regulatory designation | USFWS designated critical habitat for threatened and endangered species, **final and proposed**, as FWS's own map (two tile caches, at every height). A click on the ground answers which designations contain the point: the species, its listing status, whether the designation is final or **proposed** (always said), the Federal Register rule and the date it was published; a point in none says so, and says that this is not a finding that no listed species is there. Regulatory polygons only, never occurrence points: no GBIF, iNaturalist or NatureServe record is read. Many listed species have no designation; FWS says its GIS files are not the legal boundary (the Federal Register rule and 50 CFR 17 are). **NOAA Fisheries' marine and anadromous species** (whales, sea turtles, corals, salmon and steelhead) are drawn from NOAA's own map over FWS's and answered in the same click, with NOAA named: polygons containing the point, and river and stream lines within about 200 m (said as such), with unit, Federal Register rule, eCFR section and dates. NOAA's polygon layer is dated 2023-05-02 in its name but holds rules to July 2025; its line layer ends in 2017; the eCFR text is the legal boundary. The same click lists, separately, the **listed and proposed species whose range covers the point** (USFWS and NOAA ranges as EPA's Office of Pesticide Programs publishes them), grouped by status with taxonomic group, agency and range date: a range is where a species could occur, **not presence**, and the ranges are never drawn. A source that does not answer is said to be unknown, not absent. | USFWS `USFWS_Critical_Habitat` FeatureServer (layers 0 final, 2 proposed) and the `usfws_critical_habitat_final` / `_proposed` tile caches on ArcGIS Online; NOAA Fisheries `All_NMFS_Critical_Habitat` MapServer (layers 226 and 2) |
| **Public lands** | Below 250 km: fee lands filled by **public access** (open, restricted, closed, unknown); easements, designations and proclamation boundaries outlined (an easement's owner keeps the land and the holder has the rights the easement grants; the land inside a proclamation boundary is not all public). **Ownership as PAD-US publishes it**: owner and owner type, local owner, manager and manager type, and for easements the holder and holder type, with designation, GAP status, IUCN category and acres. Coded values are decoded with the service's own domains; the easement holder type has no domain on this service, so its published code is shown with the agency-type label beside it. Zoomed out past 60 km, the 600 largest units in the 2° box are kept, generalised to about 250 m, and parts of a unit outside the box or smaller than that are not drawn (the dossier says how many); the note says when units were left out. | USGS Gap Analysis Project, PAD-US 4.1 Manager Name service |
| **National Risk Index (FEMA)** | Below 2,500 km, every county in view in **FEMA's rating words** for the composite or any one of **18 natural hazards** (a picker under the layer's row), on the same county outlines as Jobs and Home values (joined by FIPS; Connecticut in planning regions); below 60 km, census tracts on NRI's own polygons. Click for expected annual loss in dollars, **social vulnerability as published in the NRI** (FEMA: from the Census Community Resilience Estimate, not CDC SVI), community resilience and the 18 hazards sorted by rating. A relative index: very high means high compared with everywhere else, not likely; 'Not Applicable' and 'Insufficient Data' are drawn dashed grey, never as low. This product uses FEMA's National Risk Index but is not endorsed by FEMA. | [FEMA National Risk Index](https://hazards.fema.gov/nri/), December 2025, through `/api/hazards?op=nri` (7 day server cache) |
| **Community health (CDC PLACES)** · ESTIMATE | One of **40 CDC PLACES measures** at a time (a picker under the layer's row: diabetes, obesity, smoking, checkups, depression, food insecurity, ...): below 2,500 km, every county's **age-adjusted** model-based estimate on the same county outlines, in national quintiles; below 100 km, the **crude** estimate for every published census tract of up to six counties in view. Click a county for every measure there, by CDC category. Model-based estimates, not counts of diagnosed people, and not for ranking small differences; a county or tract CDC does not publish is dashed grey, never 0. | [CDC PLACES](https://www.cdc.gov/places/) 2025 release through `/api/civic?op=health` (7 day server cache; one whole-measure pull per measure, never a per-pan query) |
| **Social vulnerability (CDC SVI)** · ESTIMATE | CDC/ATSDR's **Social Vulnerability Index 2022** (ACS 2018-2022): the overall percentile or one of CDC's four themes (a picker under the layer's row), every county below 2,500 km on the same county outlines and SVI's own census tracts below 60 km, on **fixed** breaks (0.25, 0.5, 0.75) so a county reads the same in every view. Click for the five percentiles, the flag count and CDC's 16 estimates with their margins, and directly under them, in their own block, **FEMA's NRI social vulnerability** for the same place: a different index (from the Census Community Resilience Estimate), never merged, converted or ranked against CDC's. A tract CDC does not rank (778 of 84,120) is dashed grey, never 'least'. | [CDC/ATSDR SVI](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/php/svi/index.html) 2022 through `/api/civic?op=svi`, with FEMA's row from `/api/hazards?op=nri&fips=` (7 day server cache) |
| **Broadband availability (FCC)** | The FCC's **Broadband Data Collection, December 2025**, as Esri summarizes it: every county below 2,500 km on the same county outlines, coloured by the **unserved**, **underserved** or **fiber-served share** of Broadband Serviceable Locations or by the number of **providers** (a picker under the layer's row), on fixed breaks; below 60 km, BDC's own **H3 hexagons** (about 0.7 km2; counts as published, including hexagons with one location). Click for the locations in each FCC tier with the tier definitions, by technology, providers and the unserved count a year earlier. What providers report as **available**, not what anyone subscribes to or measures; satellite and unlicensed fixed wireless count as unserved. Connecticut is published in its old counties, so its planning regions read "not published in this geography" until the hexagons load. | [FCC BDC](https://broadbandmap.fcc.gov/) December 2025 via [Esri Policy Maps](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=e1343efcefc344709057260ee57290a0) (Esri Master License Agreement) through `/api/civic?op=broadband` (7 day server cache) |
| **Wildfire risk ranks (USFS)** · MODELLED | USDA Forest Service **Wildfire Risk to Communities** (2nd edition, 2024): every county below 2,500 km and every community (Census place) below 150 km, coloured by its **national or state percentile rank** for one of four measures named by the service's own aliases (RISK, burn probability, wildfire hazard potential, exposure-adjusted risk), on fixed breaks. Click for every rank as published ("97.20%"), buildings and housing units and the shares in direct and indirect exposure. Relative and modelled: not a forecast and not a local assessment. DC and the 484 communities USFS excludes (no people, no buildings) are dashed grey, never low. ## Parcels & ownership

One layer, **Parcels**, and one route. Below 2 km the layer draws lot lines from the county or state parcel service for the ground in view, inside a dashed box that says what was loaded. Below 5 km, a click on the ground (or on a lot line, or on a zoning district with Zoning on too) asks for the parcel at that point and opens its record as the source publishes it: parcel id, site address, owner names (or why there are none), mailing address, values each with the source's own label and year, exemption, use, zoning, year built, area with its basis, last sale, legal description, the Public Land Survey section from BLM where there is one, the USDOT National Address Database points on the parcel, and the source's own record page wherever it publishes one. Keyless, and each record relayed as published: nothing is added to it and nothing is joined to it from another source.

Owners appear only in the record of the one parcel you click. Lot lines carry the parcel id and use class and nothing else, so panning across a town collects no names. There is no search from a name to what someone owns: the route takes a point or a box and refuses `owner`, `name`, `q`, `where`, `search` and `query` outright, the ⌘K palette searches a parcel's id and never its owner, taxpayer or mailing fields, and no query is ever built from an owner field.

**What is withheld, and why.** Only what a source itself suppresses, handled so the suppression holds:

- An owner field that marks the name as withheld is shown as withheld, and the mailing address goes with it. Sources write the marker in their own words ("CURRENT OWNER", "Current Co-Owner", "SUPPRESSED OWNER", "Name Withheld", a name followed by "(REDACTED)"), so the test is by word: any owner, co-owner, care-of or taxpayer name with CONFIDENTIAL, REDACTED, SUPPRESSED or WITHHELD in it as a word, or that is exactly "CURRENT OWNER" or "CURRENT CO-OWNER". An entity whose name contains one of those words is withheld too, which errs on the safe side. This word test is the only guard for Texas confidential owners (Tax Code 25.025) in the StratMap counties, since StratMap carries no confidentiality flag.
- An owner field that holds a placeholder that is not a name and not a withholding marker ("UNKNOWN OWNER", "OWNER UNKNOWN", "NAME NOT AVAILABLE") is shown as not published.
- HCAD marks confidential accounts in `confidential_flag` and replaces the owner with "CURRENT OWNER", but its service still sends the mailing address. This app treats the flag as the source's own suppression: owner withheld, mailing address and new-owner date left out.
- New Jersey's Daniel's Law restricts disclosing the home addresses of the people it covers, and NJOGIS removes owner names from its parcel services. This app goes one step further than NJOGIS: it never asks the New Jersey service for the owner mailing fields it still carries, since those can be a protected person's home, and it shows no New Jersey owner from any other source.
- Owner-occupancy flags (homestead, owner-occupied, principal-residence share) are never asked for, including a class field that encodes one: Maricopa's legal class (3.1 is a primary residence, 4.2 a rental) and Minnesota's use class (in many counties the homestead classification itself) are left out. Every adapter asks for an explicit field list (`lib/parcels/adapters.ts`, tested against a payload captured from each service), never `*`.
- NAD's licence says the database is not intended for use as a mailing list; the address points appear one parcel at a time.

Nothing a source suppresses is looked up anywhere else. A record that changes at its source (a new confidentiality flag, a sale) changes here within about a day, when its cached copy expires.

**Asking for a record to be withheld here.** If a record is about you and you do not want it shown in this app, open an issue on this repository that says only that you want a parcel record withheld: no name, no address, no parcel id. Issues here are public and show the GitHub account that opened them. A maintainer will reply with a private way to send the details. A request is honoured by adding the parcel's id to `lib/parcels/suppressed.ts`; the dossier then says a record at that point is withheld on request, and the source's own record is untouched. A legal suppression (a Texas confidentiality request to the appraisal district, a Daniel's Law request) belongs with the source, and this app follows the source's flag.

**Politeness.** One request per second per parcel host, a five-minute pause for a host after a 429 or a 503, and caches: a parcel record for a day, a box of lot lines for a week, a county lookup for 30 days, NAD points for a week. NAD's hosted layer is slow (seven seconds for an 80 m radius when probed), so a click waits 2.5 s for it and the dossier asks again separately when it has not answered.

What was checked before shipping (2026-09-26): every adapter answered a point query at a publicly owned parcel with its explicit field list, and each payload is a test fixture. Lot-line boxes the layer asks for answered in 0.4–1.4 s at most sources, 3.3 s in Albany, 9.9 s in Tallahassee and 23 s in Columbus; a box downtown returned 499 parcels around the Alamo, 746 around Houston City Hall and 751 around the Montana Capitol, and the record limit was reached in downtown Chicago, Minneapolis, Albany, Trenton, Annapolis and Boston, where the layer note says to zoom in. ## Constructs: the world laid over the world

The layers above show the physical world. **Constructs** shows the frames laid over it, and ties every point of view together through the one thing they share: location. Switch it on and the camera target grows a tethered spiral of the constructs it sits inside, smallest nearest the ground. In downtown Austin that is 31 of them: flood zone, tract, ZIP code area, state house district, subwatershed, school district, city, county, NWS zone and office, subbasin, ecoregions, metro area, congressional district, basins up to the Texas-Gulf water region, state, census division and region, EPA and FEMA regions, Federal Reserve district, time zone, country. Each is coloured by the question it answers (who governs it, who speaks for it, who serves it, how it is counted, where its water goes, what grows there, what threatens it, which federal office answers for it). Select one and its outline floats at its height over a dashed footprint on the ground, and the panel **joins it to everything loaded on the globe inside it**: the gauges in a watershed, the companies in a congressional district, the wells in a county. Select any gauge, well, company or aircraft and the panel lists the constructs it sits in.

![Travis County selected in the constructs layer: its outline floats over a dashed ground footprint, with 40 gauges and two companies joined inside it](docs/screenshot-constructs.jpg)

**Emergence.** A construct has no sensors of its own, so its state comes from the physical twin. *Construct field* tiles the whole view with one point of view's constructs and floats them as a plate above the globe. Which kind emerges depends on the zoom: water regions from orbit give way to subwatersheds over a county, states to counties to cities, congressional to state house districts. Each unit's column of light and colour rise with the physical signals loaded inside it (gauges, aircraft, ships, earthquakes, company headquarters, bank offices), recomputed every few seconds. Switch the heat to **streamflow vs normal** and the field shows what its rivers are *doing* rather than where the instruments are: every gauge's latest flow is placed among the USGS daily-mean percentiles for today's date, and each watershed takes the median of its gauges, coloured in the WaterWatch classes from much below normal (red) through normal (green) to much above (blue), its column rising with the distance from normal either way. **Where the water goes** walks the USGS drainage links from any point to the ocean and draws the path, then joins every gauge and well along it (downtown Austin reaches Matagorda Bay in 29 subwatersheds). **What drains here** reads the same links backwards: the whole catchment of a point as the smallest set of whole watersheds (downtown Austin: 832 subwatersheds, 90,411 km², drawn as 16 subbasins, 38 watersheds and 78 subwatersheds), then reads the gauges inside it near the outlet against normal for the day, so you see what is coming down the river toward you. **Live warnings** are the constructs that are born and die: every NWS alert rated Severe or Extreme, drawn as the area the forecaster drew (or the zones they named), breathing while it is in force and fading as it expires; select one to join it to the gauges and everything else inside it. **Teleport** (top bar, or say "teleport") flies to wherever the world is doing something unusual right now, NWS warnings and M4.5+ earthquakes in the order the agencies rate them, and lands in the constructs world with the right layers on; autoplay tours them. Link parameters: `lens` (who is looking; sets layers, panel and camera unless the link carries its own), `lat`, `lon`, `h` (camera height in metres), `hd` / `p` (heading, pitch), `layers` (comma list of layer ids, exactly these on), `t` (mission clock, ISO; omitted while live), `sel=layer:id` (selected object, best effort once its feed loads), `report=1` (water report open), `market=1` (market report open), `space=1` (space-weather panel open), `shape=a:lon,lat;lon,lat;…` or `shape=l:…` (a drawn area or line, up to 60 vertices, shown with its measurement; written last and unescaped so it stays readable), `ground=lat,lon` (a ground-here answer asked at that point once the camera is there, of the picture layers the link turns on), `terrain=1.5` (3D terrain on at that vertical exaggeration, 1 to 3), `slr=3` (the sea level rise scenario in feet, 1 to 10; written while that layer is on), `aoi=1` (the Area panel open on the drawn area), `here=lat,lon` (a what's-here answer asked at that point once the camera is there), `parcel=lat,lon` (the parcel identified at that point, 5 decimals; turns the parcels layer on and opens its record once the camera arrives), `zoning=lat,lon` (the zoning answer at a clicked point, 5 decimals; the Zoning layer switches on and the point's dossier opens once the camera is there), `embed=1` (no HUD chrome, for iframes; data credits stay).

Water boxes are clamped to 4° and snapped to a 0.5° grid, economy boxes to 18° and a 1° grid, fire boxes to a 1° grid, flood and wetland boxes to 0.08° and a 0.02° grid, public-land boxes to 2° and a 0.25° grid, parcel outline boxes to 0.01° and a 0.002° grid (a parcel point to 5 decimals), zoning outline boxes to 0.04° and a 0.0025° grid, permit boxes to 0.03°, licence boxes to 0.02° (both on the 0.0025° grid), environmental boxes to 0.2° and a 0.05° grid, so nearby callers share a cache entry. Zoning answers are held a day, permits 30 minutes and licences an hour (Los Angeles, which updates weekly and monthly, 6 hours), environmental permits 6 hours. Responses carry `source`, `cacheAge` and, for the report, `globe`: the permalink that opens the same point on the globe. The report from the API has no satellite turbidity term (that runs in a browser) and says so in `caveats`. Please keep the upstreams' terms in mind; the route already rate-gates and backs off on their behalf.

## Quick start

```bash
git clone https://github.com/jcdavis131/gods-eye-view
cd gods-eye-view
npm install        # also copies Cesium's static assets into public/cesium
npm run dev        # http://localhost:3000
```

That is the whole setup. No account, no token, no `.env`. Everything below the fold works on free public endpoints.

Pinokio users: add this folder (or the repo URL) in Pinokio and press **Install**, then **Start**. `pinokio.js`, `install.js`, `start.js`, `update.js` and `reset.js` are in the repo root.

## Controls

- Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, middle-drag or ctrl-drag to tilt.
- Click any object for its dossier; **Follow** locks the camera to it (your orbit offset is kept while it moves). With **Zoning** on and the camera below 15 km, a click on the ground (or a district) opens the zoning at that point instead; with **Parcels** on too, below 5 km that click opens the parcel (the Parcels bullet below).
- `⌘K` / `Ctrl+K` search flights, ships, satellites and cameras by callsign, name, MMSI, ICAO hex or NORAD id, or geocode a place. It matches names and published identifiers (a parcel id among them) and place names only (a permit, licence or action number, a zoning code), never an owner, taxpayer, operator, manager, easement-holder, licensee, applicant or contractor field, nor an address.
- With **Parcels** on and the camera below 5 km, a click on the ground opens the record of the parcel under it; lot lines draw below 2 km. With **Zoning** on too, parcels win every click on the ground below 5 km, including a click inside a drawn zoning district or on its outline (districts draw below 4 km); zoning answers from 5 to 15 km. Switch Parcels off to read zoning up close. Any other object picked (an aircraft, a ship, a camera, a power line) still opens its own dossier. A parcel or zoning click comes before a terrain, soil or land cover picture's: turn Parcels and Zoning off (whichever are on) to ask the ground what is under it.
- **Explore** opens curated places, the guided tour and exports; **Share** copies a permalink to exactly this view.
- With a terrain, soil or land cover picture on, a click on the ground opens what is under it (soil, hazard class, land cover, slope, elevation). Settings → Display turns on 3D terrain and sets its exaggeration.
- Right-click the globe for **what's here** at that point. With a drawn area, Measure's *What's inside* opens the **Area** panel (inside, watch, land report).

- **Measure** (ruler) turns clicks into a path (with an elevation profile), an area or an elevation reading; `Esc` leaves the tool and keeps the shape, which travels in the share link. **Space** (sun) opens the space-weather panel.

Why proxies at all? OpenSky locks CORS to its own origin, adsb.lol and Overpass send no CORS headers, Nominatim requires a User-Agent browsers cannot set, Digitraffic requires gzip plus a client header, and CelesTrak asks for at most one fetch per group every two hours. The routes pass upstream JSON through untouched (big payloads are trimmed and say so with `trimmed: true`), so the network tab is the audit trail. The hazards and land routes add their own reasons: the FIRMS files are about 19 MB of CSV that the server cuts to the view, the NWS alert feed is 2.8 MB trimmed to what the layer shows, FEMA resets connections and needs retries, a 2° PAD-US box has to be capped and trimmed to stay under the platform's response limit, and the ISS stream has to pass its oEmbed check before anything is embedded. The parcels route exists so that county servers see one polite, cached request per parcel or box rather than one per visitor, and so that no owner name leaves the server except in the record of the one parcel clicked. The zoning and permits routes add theirs: the Corps' ORM search sends no CORS headers, ECHO answers in two steps (a query id, then its rows, which are what is cached), the city is picked from a Census place lookup before any zoning service is asked, and every portal is asked for named columns, so no mailing address, phone, applicant or contact name is requested.

Open the console and type `gev` for a live handle: `gev.globe.getState()`, `gev.features()`, `gev.say("show me ships near Helsinki")`, `gev.run("set_time", { offset_minutes: -60 })`.

## Layer API

A layer is one file in `lib/layers/` exporting a `LayerDefinition`:

```ts
import type { LayerDefinition, FetchContext, FetchResult } from "./types";

export const myLayer: LayerDefinition = {
  id: "mything",                 // add to LayerId in lib/layers/types.ts
  label: "My thing",
  description: "Where it comes from and what it means.",
  color: "#7CFFB2",
  updateIntervalMs: 30_000,
  defaultEnabled: false,
  viewDependent: true,           // refetch when the camera settles somewhere new
  viewKey: (view) => `${view.lon.toFixed(1)},${view.lat.toFixed(1)}`, // optional, coarse by default
  simulated: false,              // true ONLY for simulations; shown loudly in the UI
  timeDependent: false,          // true to refetch when the mission clock changes day (satellite scenes)
  dependsOn: ["earthquakes"],     // optional: other layers refine() reads; a change in one re-runs refine, not fetch
  estimate: undefined,           // short caption when the layer computes rather than relays; shows an ESTIMATE tag
  attribution: "Upstream name (licence)",
  async fetch(ctx: FetchContext): Promise<FetchResult> {
    // ctx.view = camera target/height/bbox, ctx.keys = optional operator keys,
    // ctx.now = wall clock, ctx.missionTime = timeline clock, ctx.signal = abort,
    // ctx.options = settings prefs
    const res = await fetch(`/api/mything?...`, { signal: ctx.signal });
    const data = await res.json();
    return {
      collection: { type: "FeatureCollection", features: [/* GeoJSON Features with BaseProps */] },
      source: "upstream name",
      fetchedAt: Date.now(),
      note: "coverage caveat shown in the HUD",
    };
  },
  // optional: what of the fetched result to draw given the dependsOn layers. ctx.layersOn = on/off,
  // ctx.answering = has an answer on the map (not failed, not still loading), ctx.holds(layer, id) =
  // that layer holds the feature now. Runs on each fetch and on each change in those layers.
  refine: (result, ctx) => result,
};
```

Every feature carries `BaseProps` (`lib/layers/types.ts`): `id`, `layer`, `name`, optional `kind`, `heading`, `altitude`, `speed`, `observedAt`, `source`, `simulated`, `details` (rendered verbatim in the info panel), `imageUrl`, `extra`.

Register it in `lib/layers/index.ts` and give it a `LayerStyle` in `lib/globe/styles.ts`:

```ts
export const myStyle: LayerStyle = {
  color: "#7CFFB2",
  icon: (f) => "dot",                       // glyph from lib/globe/icons.ts, or null for a point
  label: (f) => f.properties.name,
  labelMax: 60,                             // label everything while the layer is small
  trail: true,                              // keep a position history for the selected object
  position: (f, timeMs) => [lon, lat, alt], // optional: moving objects
  selectedLines: (f, timeMs) => [{ positions, color, width }], // optional: orbit / track
  lines: (f) => [{ positions, color, alpha }],      // optional: polylines (rivers, roads); Multi* geometries work
  polygons: (f) => [{ rings, color, alpha }],       // optional: filled ground polygons (drought classes)
  overlay: (features) => ({ image, west, south, east, north }), // optional: one raster over the collection
  tiles: ({ meta }) => [{ key, kind: "xyz", url, maximumLevel, alpha, z, credit, maxHeight }], // optional: a publisher's tiled picture (lib/globe/tiles.ts)
  labelAlways: (f) => f.properties.kind === "major", // optional: keep a label regardless of labelMax
};
```

Rules the codebase keeps: never invent a value the upstream did not send (unknown heading stays `undefined`, AIS sentinels 511/360 are dropped), never count simulated features as live, never let a key reach third-party JavaScript except the SDK it belongs to.

## Ethics guardrails

This project shows **public infrastructure, public land and public events**. It is a way to look at the world's shared systems, not at people.

- No face recognition, no person tracking, no named-individual search, no licence plates, no phone signals. No code path identifies a person from a face, a track, a licence plate or a phone signal, and no search goes from a person's name to what they own; none will be merged.
- Parcels, addresses and ownership are in scope, and as much of them as the public sources publish: they come as each source publishes them (a county parcel service, a federal land inventory such as PAD-US with its owners, managers and easement holders), with that source's own fields and terms, and nothing is added. What stays out is the search from an owner to its holdings: ⌘K never reads an owner, operator, manager or easement-holder field, so an owner's name finds land only where the publisher's own name for the unit contains it (a PAD-US unit name as PAD-US writes it). The palette searches feature names and an explicit list of published identifiers, categories and place names (callsign, registration, MMSI, NORAD id, fire id, ticker, sector, county…; `lib/search/allowlist.ts`, tested). Institutions (public companies, banks) are found by the names their regulators publish, and a bank branch's name includes its bank ("Wells Fargo Bank · CONGRESS AVENUE BRANCH", from FDIC's institution and office names), so typing a bank's name in ⌘K, in voice "find" or in the desk table's filter lists the loaded branches of that bank. The desk table's filter matches every column of the economy layers it shows, a branch's bank among them; none of those layers carries an owner of land.
- Permits, licences and environmental permits come as their registries publish them. Every portal is asked for named columns, and no applicant, owner, contact or filing-representative name is asked for (Chicago's fifteen permit contacts, New York's applicant and owner, Austin's contractor and applicant names, a phone or mailing address anywhere); the Corps' applicant strings, which embed personal names, are never kept. Some names that can be a person's are requested or shown, and these are they: a licence registry's legal or registrant name (NY SLA, Chicago, San Francisco, Los Angeles, NYC DCWP) is requested only to test whether it is a company's and whether the trade name is that same name, and is discarded on the server otherwise; the contractor Austin, Seattle and Denver print on a permit, and the project name the Corps gives an action, are shown in the dossier as published and can be a person's name (a Corps project name that opens "Surname, Given" is withheld). Licences are shown by trade name, except a trade name that is a non-company registrant's own name, and a licence at an apartment or unit address is withheld as a home-business heuristic, counted and called a heuristic. Owner and officer registries (Chicago's Business Owners, Texas TDLR, Washington L&I principals) are not read. Features are named by their permit, licence or action number (a licence by its trade name), and ⌘K reads none of the address, contractor, facility, project or licensee fields. Zoning is regulation of land, as each city publishes it; where a city has no zoning (Houston) the dossier says so, and where no polygon covers a point it never says "unzoned".
- Cameras are limited to operator-published open data (transport agencies, public webcams). Only their published position is used; **all camera poses are coarse position estimates and no orientation is drawn** because none is published.
- The traffic simulation (vehicles on real roads driven by an aggregate demand curve) is no longer offered on the globe, because it is not a public record. Its code stays in `lib/layers/traffic.ts`; it never ingested real vehicle, phone or plate data, and an old link with `layers=traffic` simply ignores it.
- No fabricated fallbacks. When a feed is unavailable the layer says so; when coverage is partial (AIS without a key is the Baltic Sea) the layer says that too.
- Severities are the publisher's own: NWS severity, GDACS alert level, FIRMS confidence on each instrument's scale, WFIGS percent contained. When a source rates nothing, the feature says "not rated by source" in its own colour; an unknown is never drawn as calm.
- Aircraft with privacy programmes (PIA/LADD) appear only as their upstream publishes them; this app adds no de-anonymisation.
- Economic layers are about places and institutions, not people. Counties, metros, states, harbours, ports of entry, and public companies, banks and federal awards as their regulators publish them: SEC EDGAR filings and XBRL facts at a company's registered business address, FDIC institutions and branch deposits, USAspending recipients and agencies. The one exception is the people who file Forms 3, 4 and 5 about a listed company (its officers, directors and 10% owners): they may be listed only with the company they file about, by CIK, under the rules in [docs/PEOPLE.md](docs/PEOPLE.md), with no address, no search by name and no place on the map. No other shareholder names. Zillow indexes are labelled as model estimates; the affordability, momentum, HHI and per-job lines print their formulas; the sector-to-ETF bridge is a stated convention. None of it is investment, lending or relocation advice.
- Sensitive places stay as protected as their publishers keep them. Endangered species appear only as the regulatory critical habitat polygons USFWS and NOAA Fisheries publish (final and proposed), never as occurrence points or sightings from any source. Historic places come only from the National Park Service's public National Register data, which leaves out listings whose locations are restricted (most archaeological sites, under NHPA section 304 and ARPA), and the few boundary records NPS still marks "Address Restricted" are filtered out in the query; no state site file, SHPO inventory or third-party archaeological database is read, nothing is added to NPS's points, no address is requested, and a property's name (which can be a person's) is shown in the record and as the globe label as NPS lists it, but is never a search key. Every historic-places answer says that absence on the map is not absence on the ground.
- Leaking underground storage tanks are shown only as county counts. UST Finder's tank and release sites are never requested: many are private homes, their locations are home addresses, and no field marks a residential site reliably.
- PFAS results are shown per public water system, as EPA publishes them, never per tap or well and never as a pin: on the system's service area or as a ring at EPA's estimated location. A water system's name (some are apartment complexes, nursing homes or rental parks) is shown in the record and on the label only and never searched; no facility or sample-point name is requested.
- EPA's contaminated-site listings are shown as program statuses (Superfund, TRI, RCRA, Brownfields), never as a finding that a place is contaminated; EPA's facility names (which can be a sole proprietor's name or a street address) are shown in the record only and are never searched, and no address or contact field is requested. Superfund site boundaries are drawn as EPA regions map them, a regulatory area and not a contamination footprint; the EPA staff contact name, email and phone on every boundary are never requested, and a site's name is never searched. USACE's Formerly Used Defense Sites come without the current-owner field (it holds private individuals' names, P.O. boxes and phone numbers) and without the narratives; a property, site or project name can carry a private surname ("BARTLETT PROPERTY"), so it is shown in the record and on the label only and never searched or embedded, and a property is found by its FUDS property number.
- No active scanning. Every upstream is read with plain, cached, rate-gated requests under an honest User-Agent; nothing here probes hosts.

If you build on this, keep the list above intact. It is the point.

## What eye measures

One number: searches in a week that open a cited record. It is counted with Vercel Web Analytics, which sets no cookies, and it is **off until Web Analytics is enabled for the project in the Vercel dashboard**; until then the script is not served and nothing is sent.

- Page views carry the path only. eye keeps its state (layers, the view box, a selection) in the query string and the fragment, and both are stripped before anything leaves the browser (`stripUrl` in `lib/analytics/events.ts`). Vercel's script also reports the referrer, the outside page a visitor came from, as that page sent it; eye does not alter it.
- `search`, once each time the ⌘K palette closes after a query: `results`, a bucket (`0`, `1-3`, `4-8`, `9+`), and `kind`, which result groups showed (`objects`, `meaning`, `catalog`, `plss`, `place`, joined by `+`, or `none`).
- `result_open`, when a palette row is opened: `group`, the layer id from eye's own list or `catalog`, `plss`, `place`, `other`; and `source`, always `palette`.
- `record_open`, when a record's dossier opens: `layer`, a layer id from eye's own list, and `via`, `search` (a palette result or a catalogue card from it) or `map` (a click on the globe: an object, a parcel, a zoning district). A parcel or zoning click counts when it is made, even if nothing is found under it, and records opened from the side panels (screener, watchlist, area, reports) are not counted, so `map` is approximate. `catalog` is reserved; the catalogue page sends nothing of its own.

No query text, no query length, no coordinates and no record ids are sent, ever. Every event goes through one typed wrapper that drops anything off those lists, and a test feeds an address-shaped query through every helper and checks it never reaches a payload.

## Development notes

- Next.js 16 (Turbopack), React 19, Tailwind 4, shadcn (base-ui), Zustand, TanStack Query, CesiumJS 1.145, satellite.js 7.
- `scripts/copy-cesium.mjs` copies Cesium's Workers/Assets/Widgets/ThirdParty into `public/cesium` on install/dev/build; `window.CESIUM_BASE_URL` is set before Cesium is dynamically imported.
- Satellite positions come from `lib/globe/sat.worker.ts` (a Web Worker fed mission time once a second, or immediately after a timeline jump); the style falls back to main-thread SGP4 where Workers are unavailable.
- `next.config.ts` aliases `@spz-loader/core` (Cesium's Gaussian-splat decoder, unused here) to `lib/vendor/spz-loader-stub.ts`. Its inlined WebAssembly string gets minified into an invalid template literal in Turbopack production builds, which made the whole Cesium chunk fail to parse on Vercel while dev mode worked. Drive the production build in a browser, not just `curl`, before shipping.
- satellite.js is imported through `lib/vendor/satellite.ts`, not its package root: the root re-exports a WASM/pthreads runtime that imports `node:worker_threads`, which makes `next build` hang under Turbopack and fail under webpack. The shim reaches the pure SGP4 modules by relative path.
- The turbidity layer never touches the server: `lib/water/turbidity.worker.ts` does the STAC search and the COG range reads (geotiff.js) inside a module Worker, so the multi-megabyte band windows stay in the browser. Overview level follows camera height (10 m below 40 km, 20 m below 100 km, 40 m below 200 km, else 80 m) and a chip needs the same water-pixel fraction the explainer required (25 of 64×64 at 10 m).
- `lib/water/dogliotti.ts` is the whole algorithm; `lib/water/quality.ts` holds the thresholds and the screening index; `lib/water/report.ts` the community report. All three are plain functions with no Cesium or React in them so they can be unit-tested or reused.
- `node scripts/turbidity-check.mjs <lon> <lat> [resolution] [before-date]` runs the very same worker code from Node (compiled on the fly into `.tmp-turb/`) and prints the scene it chose, the DN→ρ check and the chips. `node scripts/turbidity-check.mjs -98.34 29.28 20 2025-01-19` reproduces the explainer's Bexar scene: Calaveras chips 4.3–4.9 FNU, Braunig 3.9–4.0.
- Sentinel-2 COG gotchas met on the way: only the first IFD carries a geotransform, so an overview's origin/resolution must be derived from the base image (geotiff.js throws "no affine transformation" otherwise); the STAC `raster:bands` offset of −0.1 does not match this bucket's pixels (DN/10000 does), which the worker verifies per scene; and a tile's `eo:cloud_cover` says little about one lake, so scenes are ranked by cloud over the actual window.
- `node scripts/hud-shots.mjs <outDir> '<scenarios>'` screenshots the HUD at phone, tablet and desktop sizes against a running `next start` (needs `npm i --no-save playwright-core` and a Chromium; see the script header). Use it before touching the layout.
- `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm test` (vitest; `*.test.ts` next to the code, network-free with fixtures), `npm run build`.
- CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs `tsc --noEmit`, vitest, eslint on the changed files and the freeze check on every pull request and every push to master, on GitHub's runners. `FREEZE.json` holds the catalogue at 60 collections until 2026-11-01: `node scripts/freeze-check.mjs` counts the entries in `lib/catalog/collections.ts` and fails, naming the new ids, if there are more before that date. It only blocks a merge once Cam marks the check required in branch protection.
- Server state: `lib/series/store.ts` picks the series store (file under `data/series`, the raw GitHub adapter when `GEV_SERIES_RAW_BASE` is set, memory in tests). Environment variables the practitioner routes read: `GEV_SERIES_DIR`, `GEV_SERIES_RAW_BASE`, `GEV_CRON_SECRET` (enables `POST /api/series?op=collect` and the watchlist webhook ops), `GEV_WATCH_KV`, `GEV_WATCH_DIR`, `GEV_PUBLIC_ORIGIN`, `GEV_BASE_URL` (MCP), `AISSTREAM_KEY` (snapshot cron, optional), `GEV_INDICATORS_NO_STORE`. None is required.
- Upstream shapes for SEC EDGAR, FDIC BankFind, USAspending, AISStream and the TIGERweb ZCTA layer were written from their published documentation and fixture-tested; the sandbox this build ran in had no egress to those hosts, so the first live run should be watched (each parser fails loudly rather than inventing a value).
- Dev and production builds use separate output directories, so `next build` can run while `next dev` is up.
- `next.config.ts` sets the security headers (CSP, nosniff, referrer, Permissions-Policy). Three of the app's own features constrain it, each verified by `security-headers.test.ts`: `script-src` needs `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` or Cesium cannot instantiate its WebAssembly and the globe never draws (the HUD still renders over a black screen, which reads as a slow network rather than a header); `Permissions-Policy` keeps `microphone=(self)` for voice control; and `?embed=1` gets its own rule with `frame-ancestors https:` and no `X-Frame-Options`, since that view is documented for iframes while every other request stays `'none'`/`DENY`. The two rules are exact complements (`has` vs `missing` on the same query match), so a request gets one and never both.
- `scripts/patch-knockout-csp.mjs` rewrites the `(0,eval)("this")` in the Knockout copy vendored inside `@cesium/widgets` to `globalThis`, on install/dev/build. Bundled as an ES module that expression always runs, and under the CSP above it throws during module evaluation, taking the whole Cesium chunk down with it. The rewrite is a runtime no-op and lets the policy stay free of `'unsafe-eval'`.
- The project was renamed from God's Eye View to Embedding Atlas. The internal `gev` namespace stayed put on purpose, because renaming it breaks live installs rather than just labels: the `GEV_*` environment variables (a rename silently disables the features that read them until every host is updated), the `gev:` localStorage keys (a rename drops each visitor's saved lens and desk notes), the `x-gev-cron-secret` header, the `gev://docs/` MCP resource URIs, the `gev-snapshot` provenance source id (part of the published response contract) and `examples/gev.py`. The console API is now `window.atlas`, with `window.gev` kept as an alias. The GitHub repository is still `jcdavis131/gods-eye-view`, so every repository and raw-content URL in the code and docs still points there.
- Route handlers keep a process-local TTL cache (`lib/server/cache.ts`) and a per-upstream politeness gate (`lib/server/upstream.ts`). The cache also takes a deadline (stop waiting, keep the value when it lands) and a cool-down (fail fast after a failure), which the hazards and land routes use so one slow upstream never holds up the others.
- `lib/server/arcgis.ts` is the one ArcGIS REST query helper (FEMA, USFWS, PAD-US, WFIGS, TIGERweb): it turns ArcGIS's 200-with-`{ error }` answers into errors. It and every other hazards / land / space fetcher go through `retrying()` in `lib/server/net.ts`: connection resets, timeouts and 5xx are retried with a growing pause, 4xx and 429 never. FEMA's NFHL reset 2 of 3 export requests in probing. The same three routes also call `preferIpv4()`: on the machine this was built on, the IPv6 path to CloudFront-fronted hosts (USGS EPQS, earthquake.usgs.gov) reset connections under Node's fetch while IPv4 was clean. It sets Node's DNS order for the whole server process, so under a local `next start` every route resolves IPv4 first once one of those three has loaded. None of the upstreams is IPv6-only. Whether Vercel's egress sees the same resets was not tested.
- The hazard and land builders are plain functions (`lib/hazards/features.ts`, `lib/land/features.ts`, `lib/space/weather.ts`), tested next to them; the network code lives in `lib/hazards/sources.ts` and `lib/space/sources.ts`, route handlers only. FIRMS files are parsed once per 30 minutes into typed arrays (about 100k rows per VIIRS file) and each request only filters and bins them.
- Picture layers (`lib/layers/terrain.ts`, drawn by `lib/globe/terrainStyles.ts`) are `LayerStyle.tiles` specs (`lib/globe/tiles.ts`); where each comes from is one catalog, `lib/terrain/products.ts`, which `/api/terrain?op=products` serves and the CSP test reads for the hosts the browser fetches. Cesium raises any level below a provider's `minimumLevel` to it, which from a tilted view means thousands of tiles, so no provider sets one: a product's floor is answered with a blank tile and no request. Cesium decodes imagery to an `ImageBitmap` already flipped for upload (WebGL ignores `UNPACK_FLIP_Y` for bitmaps) but flips a canvas as it uploads it, so the contour recolouring and the relief shading hand back a bitmap for a bitmap; otherwise the tile comes out upside down. USGS's relief tiles are opaque, nearly white JPEGs (flat ground about 252, open sea a flat 244), so the relief is drawn as its shading alone, black with alpha (255 − lightness) / 255, which over the imagery is a multiply: flat ground and the sea stay clear. The relief and sea level caches publish tiles only where they have them and answer 404 elsewhere (abroad past level 8 for relief, which also has holes at level 9 inside the US with levels 10–13 under them; away from US coasts for sea level), so for those two a 404 is "no tile here": Cesium keeps the nearest ancestor it has, the layer does not report tiles failing, and that tile is not asked for again while the layer is on. A 5xx, a timeout or an answer that is not an image still counts.
- Keyless terrain (`lib/terrain/terrarium.ts`) fills Cesium's geographic tiles (two at level 0, so the poles are covered) with a 65 × 65 grid sampled from the Web Mercator terrarium tiles under them, with at most eight tile requests in flight; past twice that the terrain callback answers `undefined`, which Cesium reads as "ask again later", never as "no data". A failed fetch is taken as transient: the attempt resolves `undefined` too, and the tile waits 1, 2 and then 4 s before trying again (a 429 or 503 makes every tile wait). A tile still failing after that is drawn by Cesium from its parent, except at level 0, which has no parent and without which Cesium draws nothing of that half of the globe, imagery and pictures included (both halves come from the one terrarium tile 0/0/0); a level-0 tile still failing is drawn flat, the smooth globe comes back and the log says keyless terrain is unreachable (switch 3D terrain off and on to try again). The globe is blank for at most those retries, about 7 s, as on first load. `lib/terrain/terrarium.test.ts` checks the retries and `lib/terrain/webmercator.test.ts` checks the decode against USGS EPQS at four points.
- `lib/parcels/` is the parcels layer's back end: `adapters.ts` holds one adapter per keyless county or state service (its explicit field list, its county or state, a `normalize()` into one `ParcelRecord`), `normalize.ts` the shared cleaning and privacy rules every record passes through (`finalize()`), `sources.ts` the network side (TIGERweb county lookup, point and box queries, TxGIO's identify, Cook's PIN hop, NAD, PLSS), `features.ts` the outline and dossier features, `pick.ts` the click-to-identify store and overlay, `suppressed.ts` the withheld-on-request list. Each adapter is tested against a payload captured from its own service at a publicly owned parcel (`lib/parcels/fixtures/`); rules that need a confidential or masked record are tested on those payloads with the flag or the placeholder set, and the tests say so.
- The zoning and permits routes (`app/api/zoning`, `app/api/permits`) read nine city zoning services, seven permit portals, five licence registries, EPA ECHO and the Corps' ORM through `lib/civic/request.ts`: one politeness gate per host (per organisation on ArcGIS Online, which serves many cities from one host), one request a second to Socrata portals, two to ArcGIS, one every two seconds to ECHO and ORM, and ten minutes off after a 429 or a 503. The adapters are plain functions (`lib/zoning/features.ts`, `lib/permits/{features,licences,environmental}.ts`), each tested on payloads captured from its own service with the route's own request builders; where a captured row carried a name that may be a person's, the fixture has it replaced with a `[removed from this fixture: ...]` marker, and the tests check that no such field reaches a record either way.
- The measure tool's area is exact in area for great-circle edges on the authalic sphere: `lib/globe/measure.test.ts` checks it against the WGS84 closed form computed on its own (510,065,622 km² for the whole ellipsoid; 1.075800 km² for a 0.01° cell at 29.4°N) and the line length against Cesium's geodesic loaded in Node (one degree of latitude at the equator, 110,574.4 m).

## Disclaimer

Positions are as reported by their upstream and may be delayed, dead-reckoned (aircraft/ships for ≤ 90–180 s) or propagated (satellites via SGP4). Launch ascent arcs are coarse estimates. The traffic layer is a simulation. Water-quality screens compare a gauge's latest provisional reading with published thresholds; satellite turbidity and the supply-stress number are estimates with their formulas shown, not measurements, and none of it is advice about whether water is safe to drink. The hazard layers relay what NIFC, NASA, NWS, GDACS and EONET publish, minutes to hours late; they are not a warning service, so follow official channels and local authorities in an emergency. FEMA flood zones are a regulatory map, not a forecast; NWI wetlands are mapped from imagery that can be decades old; and PAD-US public-access codes are not permission to enter. Wildfire Hazard Potential is an index for long-term fuels planning, not a map of risk or a forecast; NOAA's sea level rise layers are screening-level scenarios; soil ratings are survey interpretations, not a price or a statement about any one field. The infrastructure layers are public reference maps, some of them generalized or archived (the transmission map was last updated 2024-09-30), and none is for locating anything before digging; dam hazard potential is the consequence of a failure, not a dam's condition; Quaternary faults are not an earthquake forecast; AirNow readings are preliminary; and GDELT events are machine-coded from news reports, not verified incidents. Parcel records are tax records relayed as their county or state publishes them: not surveys, not title records, and possibly older than the source's own record, which is linked. Zoning is shown as each city's GIS publishes it, not as a zoning verification; a permit is permission to build, not proof that work happened; environmental compliance is in ECHO's own words and is only as current as ECHO. This is an educational and situational-awareness toy, not a navigation, safety or surveillance tool.
